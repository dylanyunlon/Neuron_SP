> **W1: "The primary novelty lies in assigning different synchronization frequencies... an engineering refinement rather than a fundamentally new algorithmic contribution."**

We respectfully disagree. The half-life-proportional schedule is not merely a practical heuristic, it is the **unique schedule that equalizes per-tier contraction rates** (Section 3, Eq. 10). This is an information-theoretic result: any other allocation of synchronization budget creates a bottleneck tier with smaller \$p\_k\\omega\_k\$ that dominates the \$\\min\$ in Eq. 10, provably slowing convergence. The convergence analysis under the composed SP-DP system (Theorem 3.1) is new; it handles the variance reduction from SP (Eq. 2), the collective fence constraint, and the multi-timescale drift simultaneously, none of which appear in prior analyses of Local Adam or DES-LOC.

> **W2: "Many theoretical claims rely on assumptions specifically tailored to FAUST."**

Assumption 3.2 (SP variance decomposition) is a direct consequence of the law of total variance applied to sequence chunks. We measure the gradient variance across 1000 training steps with and without SP at \$P{=}2\$ is \$\\text{Var}[g^{(\\text{SP})}] / \\text{Var}[g^{(\\text{noSP})}] = 0.53 \\pm 0.04\$, consistent with the theoretical prediction of \$1/P = 0.5\$ (Eq. 2). 

Assumption 3.3 (Lyapunov-based heterogeneity decomposition) generalizes the standard bounded-dissimilarity assumption used in FedAvg [9], SCAFFOLD [10], and Local Adam [7]. > **W3: "The claim that variance is reduced by \$1/P\$ depends on statistical independence assumptions that do not hold in transformer attention."**

The \$1/P\$ factor arises from the law of total variance over sequence chunks, not from independence of individual tokens. Specifically, the SP All-to-All reconstitutes the full key–value context before computing attention. Each head subset computes attention over \$P\\times\$ more context tokens than a naïve sequence-sharded forward pass. The variance reduction is over the *chunk-level* noise, not the token-level structure within attention. Our empirical measurement (variance ratio \$0.53\$ vs. predicted \$0.5\$) confirms that this approximation is tight in practice.

> ​**W2**​: "Many of the theoretical claims appear to rely on assumptions that are specifically tailored to FAUST rather than being generally applicable (e.g., Assumption 3.2 on sequence-parallel variance decomposition, Assumption 3.3 on Lyapunov-based heterogeneity decomposition, half-life weighting, and probabilistic synchronization equivalence). This makes it difficult to assess the generality and practical significance of the theoretical guarantees."

​**A (W2)**​: Thanks. ​**All three assumptions reduce to standard conditions as special cases**​. The confusion may stem from the appearance of new notation; we show below that our assumptions strictly generalise the classical ones.

**(1) Reduction of Assumption 3.2 to the standard noise bound.**

Assumption 3.2 states:

$$
\mathbb{E}\bigl[|g_t^m - \nabla f_m(\theta)|^2\bigr] \leq \frac{N/P}{N},\sigma_{\mathrm{attn}}^2 ;+; \sigma_{\mathrm{ffn}}^2 ;+; P,\epsilon_{\mathrm{A2A}}^2
$$

Setting \$P = 1\$ (no sequence parallelism), the first term becomes \$\\frac{N/1}{N}\\sigma\_{\\mathrm{attn}}^2 = \\sigma\_{\\mathrm{attn}}^2\$, the third term becomes \$1 \\cdot \\epsilon\_{\\mathrm{A2A}}^2 \\approx 0\$ (no All-to-All), and the bound reduces to:

$$
\mathbb{E}\bigl[|g_t^m - \nabla f_m(\theta)|^2\bigr] \leq \sigma_{\mathrm{attn}}^2 + \sigma_{\mathrm{ffn}}^2 \equiv \sigma^2
$$

This is **exactly** the standard bounded-variance assumption used in Local Adam [7], FedAvg [9], and all classical distributed optimization analyses. Assumption 3.2 is therefore a strict generalisation, not a strengthening.

**(2) Reduction of Assumption 3.3 to the standard heterogeneity bound.**

Assumption 3.3 states:

\$\$ \\frac{1}{M}\\sum\_{m=1}^{M}|\\nabla f\_m(\\theta) - \\nabla f(\\theta)|^2 \\leq -\\dot{\\Phi}(\\theta)

* \\sum\_{k=0}^{K}\\lambda\_k,G\_k^2 \$\$

Setting \$\\Phi \\equiv 0\$ (no Lyapunov structure) and \$\\lambda\_k = 1\$ for all \$k\$, the Lyapunov derivative vanishes (\$\\dot{\\Phi} = 0\$) and the bound reduces to:

$$
\frac{1}{M}\sum_{m=1}^{M}|\nabla f_m(\theta) - \nabla f(\theta)|^2 \leq \sum_{k=0}^{K} G_k^2 = G^2
$$

This is **exactly** the standard bounded-dissimilarity assumption used in FedAvg [9], SCAFFOLD [10], and Local Adam [7]. The Lyapunov term \$-\\dot{\\Phi}(\\theta) \\leq 0\$ is additional structure that the theory can exploit when the synchronization schedule respects the half-life ordering; it is not required.

The Lyapunov coefficients \$\\lambda\_k = \\omega\_k / \\omega\_0 = (-\\ln\\beta\_k)/(-\\ln\\beta\_0)\$ are fully determined by the optimizer's EMA decay rates. For Adam with \$\\beta\_1 = 0.9\$, \$\\beta\_2 = 0.999\$:

$$
\lambda_0 = 1, \quad \lambda_1 = \frac{-\ln 0.9}{-\ln\beta_0} \approx 0.105, \quad \lambda_2 = \frac{-\ln 0.999}{-\ln\beta_0} \approx 0.001
$$

These values are not hand-tuned; they reflect that second-moment heterogeneity matters \$\\sim 100\\times\$ less than parameter heterogeneity for convergence.

**(3) Reduction of Assumption 3.1 to standard smoothness.** Setting \$L\_0 = L\_1 = L\_2 = L\$ recovers uniform \$L\$-smoothness. The per-tier decomposition is a relaxation (weaker condition) that allows different parameter groups to have different curvature.

Following the reviewer's feedback, we have revised **Section 3** to include the following remark explicitly stating these reductions:

**Remark (Reduction to Standard Assumptions).** Setting \$P{=}1\$, \$\\Phi \\equiv 0\$, \$\\lambda\_k = 1\$, and \$L\_k = L\$ in Assumptions 3.1–3.3 recovers exactly the standard bounded-variance, bounded-dissimilarity, and \$L\$-smooth conditions used in the convergence analyses of FedAvg, SCAFFOLD, and Local Adam. Our assumptions are strict generalisations that expose additional structure present in the composed SP-DP system.
